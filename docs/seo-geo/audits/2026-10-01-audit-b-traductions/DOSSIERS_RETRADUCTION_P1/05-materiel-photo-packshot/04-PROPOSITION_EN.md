# The Complete Guide to Packshot Photography, Part 2: Photo Equipment and What You Need to Know

Welcome to the second installment of our complete guide to packshot photography. In this article, we cover the essentials of photo equipment for packshot photography, along with the core concepts you need to know to get the best results.

Whether you're a company looking to maximize online sales of your products, a professional just starting out, or an experienced photographer looking to sharpen your skills, this guide offers a wealth of practical and technical information to help you succeed with product photography. Proven advice, practical tips, and advanced techniques will help you get the most out of your photo equipment and produce professional-quality images.

So make yourself comfortable, grab your camera, and get ready to discover the secrets of packshot photography.

## Packshot photography equipment: what do you need to shoot your products?

### First, make sure you have enough space on your premises

Before anything else, make sure you have enough space to shoot your packshots, because photo equipment quickly takes up room. How much space you need depends largely on the method you choose:

![Traditional photo studio with a white backdrop, softboxes on stands, and ceiling-mounted lights](/images/blog/67d15a1ea67d40a54ffcc3c3.avif)

- If you want to set up a **traditional photo studio** on your premises, you'll need enough room for all your lights, a platform for your products, the background, the rest of your photo equipment, and so on.

- If you opt instead for an [**automated photo studio**](/en/besoins-photographie-produit) like the ones we sell, you'll find these studios are much more compact than a traditional photo studio.

Whichever option you choose, you'll also need space next to the studio to gather your products before the shoot, plus a work surface to unpack them, prep them, clean them if needed, and then repack them.

### Choose your photo equipment carefully, especially the camera

For a successful product packshot, it's crucial to choose the right photo equipment, especially your camera: it can make a huge difference in image quality and visual impact. In [skincare and cosmetics](/en/industrie/cosmetiques-beaute), for example, a camera with excellent color reproduction is essential to render product shades accurately.

But which camera should you choose? The range is vast, in both types and prices…

It might be tempting to stick with a smartphone, but its image quality is too limited for good packshots.

![The same cosmetic dropper bottle photographed with a smartphone on a gray background, then with an Orbitvu photo studio on a white background](/images/blog/67dbae71507d67953210f1b0.avif)

_Caption: Packshot taken with a smartphone vs. packshot taken with a specialized Orbitvu photo studio_

Instead, we recommend investing in a dedicated camera. **DSLR cameras** are widely considered the benchmark for packshot photography: they deliver high-resolution images and great versatility.
⏎ But what really matters is your camera's **sensor size and quality**, which have a significant influence on the resolution and level of detail in your photos.
⏎ The most common sensor sizes are full frame (about 35mm wide) and APS-C (about 24mm wide). [À VALIDER : même point que le FR — le capteur plein format mesure 36 × 24 mm ; remplacer « about 35mm » par « 36mm » si Sébastien le confirme — 06, point 6.] For packshot photography, we recommend **APS-C**, which offers greater depth of field and therefore a sharper product in the frame.
⏎ Beyond the camera itself, think about the rest of your photo equipment, such as lenses and tripods. In the [high-tech, household appliance, and computing sectors](/en/industrie/electronique-hightech), for example, **macro lenses** may be needed to photograph small components accurately. Your choice of lenses determines the focal length, aperture, and sharpness you get, while a tripod keeps the camera steady and limits camera shake.

### Get your lighting right

Lighting is a crucial element of a quality packshot, and one you shouldn't overlook: good lighting brings out your products' features, texture, and details, and makes them attractive to your customers.
⏎ First, the ideal lighting for product photography is **soft, even light**, free of unwanted shadows or reflections. That doesn't mean the final shot should have no shadows at all: a product without a shadow is also a product without depth. What you need to avoid is adding unnecessary shadows.
⏎ To achieve this, you have several options: use a **softbox**, place **reflectors or diffusion panels** around the product, or position lights on either side of the product, preferably with **LED bulbs**.

![The same keyboard photographed under three color temperatures: cool, neutral, and warm](/images/blog/67d15a1ea67d40a54ffcc3d2.avif)

You should also consider the **color temperature** of your lighting, which can affect how your products are perceived. For example, light that is too cool can make the image feel sterile, while light that is too warm can distort the product's colors. The recommended choice is therefore a **neutral white light** with a color temperature of **5500K**.

One more equipment tip: we recommend working with a **tripod** to avoid camera shake, especially if you use a slow shutter speed to get a brighter image.

### Choose a suitable background

In packshot photography, always choose the simplest possible background: the goal is to keep attention on the product.

![The same wooden toy car photographed on a white, a gray, and a red background](/images/blog/67d15a1ea67d40a54ffcc40b.avif)

A busy or colorful background can distract the customer's eye and detract from your product. Likewise, a gray or black background can make the product look flat.
⏎ So keep it simple and opt for a **perfectly white, plain background**. It will also make it easier to cut out the product later if you need a transparent background for other media!

### Make your shots repeatable

When you shoot packshots to showcase your products online, it's essential to pay close attention to the **repeatability and uniformity** of your shots. For your whole product range to look consistent, you need to reproduce a similar shooting setup for each product.

![Top-down setup diagram: white background, product in the center, two lamps on either side, and the camera facing the product](/images/blog/67d15a1ea67d40a54ffcc411.avif)

However, this uniformity can be hard to maintain when you work with a traditional photo studio and all the equipment that goes with it. Setting up each product can quickly become a burden and make it difficult to reach an acceptable level of uniformity.

That's why we strongly recommend **drawing up a precise setup plan** for your photo equipment, carefully recording distances, angles, and the settings used, to ensure that each shot is similar to the previous one. This rigor inevitably takes time up front, but it saves you from retouching every photo individually in post-production.

Note that with one of our automated studios, you can **save all your positions and settings** and reuse them from one photo to the next in a single click. This step then becomes child's play! You'll also have far less equipment to take out and set back up each time.

### Use good post-production software

Powerful post-production software not only lets you remove any imperfections that could affect image quality, but above all lets you adjust brightness and sharpness, crop, or remove the background: all essential steps for a perfect packshot.

With this in mind, [**Adobe Photoshop**](https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop.html) is an excellent example of post-production software for retouching packshots. It is known for its ability to process high-quality images with great precision and flexibility.

However, this type of photo software isn't something you can pick up on the fly. Mastering Photoshop's features takes time and practice: working with its many image-processing options requires a degree of expertise that only comes with experience.

It's therefore essential to plan for the learning time needed to master this kind of software. You also need to be prepared to invest time and money in training your employees to get top-quality packshots.

## How to take packshot photos: the basics you need to know

If your business wants to get started with packshot photography, it's imperative to follow certain basic rules to achieve professional results.

### Presentation

First of all, presentation must be meticulous: take the time to arrange the product carefully, position it at an angle that works with the light, and think about where it sits in the frame.

It's also crucial to **clean and prepare the product** before photographing it: stains or scratches on the item would spoil the final image. It may seem obvious, but this care can make a big difference during the shoot.

### Background

Once again, the choice of background is an important aspect of packshot photography. Ideally, use a **neutral white background**, as it showcases the product and keeps the image visually balanced. Colored backgrounds can draw attention away from the product and compromise image quality. Remember: we have no artistic ambitions here. The primary goal is to present the product faithfully.

### Sharpness

![Packshot of white sunglasses on a black background, sharp from front to back](/images/blog/67dbae71507d67953210f1b8.avif)

Finally, it's crucial to avoid blur anywhere on the product by using [**focus stacking**](/en/guide/how-to-do-focus-stacking-for-ring-photography). This technique consists of taking several shots, shifting the focus point each time, and then combining them to get an image that is sharp across the entire object. The product is thus shown exactly as it is, with full sharpness. Focus stacking is fairly time-consuming with a traditional photo studio, but it can be done automatically with an automated photo studio.

Successful, high-quality packshots therefore come down to taking care of every detail, using the right shooting techniques, and working with the appropriate retouching tools. This is also where you can see just how much an automated photo studio can simplify packshot photography, at every stage.

**What is the difference between a standard product photo and a packshot?**

A standard product photo may include decorative elements, staging, or a context of use. A packshot, on the other hand, is more technical and standardized: the product is usually isolated on a neutral background, photographed from several angles, with optimal lighting that reveals all its details and features. A packshot prioritizes precision and clarity over artistic effect.

**What is the difference between packshot, lifestyle, and still life photography?**

Packshot photography focuses on representing the product faithfully on a neutral background for e-commerce. Lifestyle photography shows the product in its context of use, while still life takes an artistic approach in service of brand communication.

**Why avoid generic supplier photos?**

Using the same photos as your competitors undermines your differentiation. An automated photo studio lets you create unique visuals that strengthen your brand identity and your customers' trust: they see exactly what they are buying.

**How many photos do you usually need for a complete packshot?**

A complete packshot typically includes a main front view, two or three views from different angles to show volume and shape, a back view to display the packaging information, and one or two detail views to highlight the product's key features. You can also add a 360-degree view made up of 24 to 72 images, depending on the level of precision you want. For e-commerce, a minimum of 5 to 7 photos is generally recommended to give a complete view of the product and reduce buyer uncertainty.

**Which lenses are recommended for packshot photography?**

Several types of lenses are useful, depending on the products you photograph. A macro lens in the 85–105mm range is ideal for capturing small products and fine details with exceptional precision. For great versatility, a standard 50mm prime lens with a good maximum aperture (f/1.8 or f/1.4) is an excellent choice for most everyday products. If you work with products of varying sizes, a professional-quality zoom such as a 24–70mm offers the flexibility to adapt quickly to different situations. In every case, prioritize lenses that deliver excellent sharpness and minimize distortion, so your products are represented accurately.
