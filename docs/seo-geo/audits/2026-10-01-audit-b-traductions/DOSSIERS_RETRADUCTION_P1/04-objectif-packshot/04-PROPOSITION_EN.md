# How to choose the best lens for product photography

The quality of a product image no longer depends only on lighting or set design: the lens now plays a central role in a successful packshot. When the precision of a single detail can make the difference between a click and an abandoned cart, choosing the right lens becomes a strategic step for any company that wants to showcase its products through imagery.

[Jewelry](/en/industrie/bijoux-joaillerie), [cosmetics](/en/industrie/cosmetiques-beaute), [textiles](/en/industrie/mode-textile), [technical parts](/en/industrie/pieces-techniques-industrie), shiny surfaces, textured materials… every product category calls for a different rendering, and therefore a suitable lens.

In this guide, PackshotCreator walks you through the major technological developments, the key selection criteria and the advanced techniques that will help you get the most out of your camera lens. We also share our experience with Orbitvu automated studios, especially the [Alphashot Pro G2](/en/studio-photo/alphashot-pro-g2), which let you unlock the full potential of these lenses in a 100% controlled workflow.

## Why the right lens has become a strategic choice in product photography

A good lens delivers an image that is not only **sharp**, but also **true to color**, with **no distortion** and **no stray reflections**. It lets you reproduce the product accurately, exactly as it is, while highlighting what makes it unique.

Packshot photography comes with its own set of constraints:

- Products are **static**, often **small**, sometimes **shiny**
- The background is usually **neutral** or **transparent**
- Shots must be **consistent** and **reproducible**, often in an **automated** setup
- The final image must work both as an **e-commerce thumbnail** and as a **large-format print**

All of these are reasons to look beyond the simple idea of a “good zoom” or a “fast lens,” and to choose, methodically, the lens best suited to your product range and your organization.

## New lens technologies and their impact on packshot photography

### Artificial intelligence and predictive autofocus

If your products are photographed **while rotating on a turntable**, some **autofocus systems** with **AI algorithms** can **anticipate and maintain focus** throughout the rotation. This advance is increasingly common in the professional lines from Canon, Sony and Nikon, and it is genuinely useful in automated photography.

These modern autofocus systems rely on artificial intelligence to **predict subject movement** and **adjust focus** accordingly. Especially useful for fast-moving or erratic subjects, this technology makes focusing more accurate, with acquisition times as short as 0.05 seconds on some high-end models.

These systems save a significant amount of time when you shoot in series, especially if you use an automated studio that runs through multiple angles and formats.

### New generations of optical glass

**Multilayer coatings** applied to lens elements (notably **anti-reflective** and **anti-flare** coatings) have made enormous progress, and so has the glass itself. The result: colored fringes along the edges of the product (chromatic aberration) are better controlled, and shiny surfaces (bottles, plastics, polished metal) are rendered more cleanly.

For packshot photography, this means more faithful images, less retouching and a faster workflow.

## Which types of lenses are used in product photography, and when should you choose each one?

### Macro lens: essential for details, textures and small objects

The macro lens remains the **go-to choice** for photographing **small objects** (jewelry, bottles, components) or highlighting **texture details** (cream, mother-of-pearl, machined metal, etc.).

![Packshot of a silver ring set with a multicolored stone, on a white background](/images/blog/67dedf2d5b1d1dd831c27c7b.avif)

With their **1:1 magnification ratio**, these lenses project a **life-size image** onto the sensor, with no loss of sharpness. This is the type of lens recommended to get the most out of **focus stacking** in automated studios, which delivers **front-to-back sharpness** even on complex shapes.

A 100mm focal length is often preferred to avoid distortion and unwanted shadows. Recommended models:

- [Sigma 105mm f/2.8 DG DN Macro Art](https://www.sigma-photo.fr/objectifs-photo/382-sigma-105mm-f28-dg-dn-macro.html)

Note: these lenses are also ideal for cosmetic textures, screw caps and anything that calls for a highly precise rendering of detail.

### Prime lens: maximum sharpness and consistent professional results

A prime lens (for example **50mm**, **85mm** or **100mm**) generally offers **better sharpness** and **less distortion** than a zoom. It is perfect for medium to large products, as long as you have enough working distance in the studio.

Without a zoom, the photographer has to adjust the distance to the product, but the angle of view stays constant, which is very useful when producing images in series.

This consistency is even more valuable in an automated studio, where every angle, distance and setting can be saved and reused exactly.

### Zoom lens: versatility and time savings for multi-product shoots

Modern zooms such as the [**Canon EF 24-105mm f/4L**](https://www.canon.fr/lenses/ef-24-105mm-f-4l-is-ii-usm-lens/) let you switch quickly from one object to another without changing lenses. For a company that photographs several product families, the time savings are significant.

Be careful, though: not all zooms are equal. Some entry-level models deliver lower sharpness or noticeable vignetting. For best results, choose **professional or semi-professional** models with a **constant aperture**.

### Wide-angle lens: best kept for bulky objects or staged shots

A wide-angle lens (focal length under 35mm) can be useful for photographing furniture, home appliances or mannequins. But it introduces **perspective distortion** (a bulging effect) that changes how the product is perceived.

![E-commerce photo of a mustard-yellow velvet three-seat sofa on a white background](/images/blog/67dedf2d5b1d1dd831c27c6f.avif)

In automated packshot photography, it is rarely used. It remains an interesting tool for lifestyle images or styled packshots, for example in home decor or sports.

## What technical criteria should you look at to choose the best lens for product photography?

### Best focal length for product photography: 50mm and up

In packshot photography, focal lengths under 50mm introduce visible distortion, especially at the edges of the frame. It is therefore best to choose a lens of **at least 50mm**, or even **85mm** or **100mm**, to get a natural perspective and enough working distance.

Note: on an APS-C camera, the field of view matches that of a focal length about 1.5 times longer. A 50mm lens then frames like a 75mm on full frame, which can be an advantage for small objects.

### Magnification ratio: capturing the finest details

A **1:1 magnification ratio** is essential to reproduce an object at life size on the sensor. It is what lets you capture every engraving, texture or reflection on a ring, a bottle or carefully crafted packaging.

![Magnification ratio diagram: at 1:1, a 2 cm coin forms a 2 cm image on the sensor; at 1:2, a 1 cm image](/images/blog/67dedf2d5b1d1dd831c27c86.avif)

Not every lens labeled “macro” is 1:1, so always check the specifications. With a 1:2 lens, the image on the sensor is half the size of the real object, which limits the level of detail in close-ups.

### Minimum focusing distance

In an automated photo studio (such as the Alphashot Pro G2), you can't always move the camera closer or farther away. That is why your lens must be able to focus at close range, without you having to back up.

Spec sheets list this distance. With a minimum focusing distance under 30 cm, you can comfortably handle most small products.

[Paragraphe APS-C en double supprimé ici, comme en FR.]

### Recommended aperture: between f/8 and f/11

An aperture of f/8 to f/11 offers a good balance between **sharpness**, **depth of field** and **exposure**. At apertures wider than f/5.6, background blur can become a problem. Beyond f/16, diffraction gradually degrades image quality.

![Close-up of a lens's aperture blades](/images/blog/67dedf2c5b1d1dd831c27c69.avif)

For very large or complex products (a handbag with a handle, a contoured bottle, etc.), consider [**automated focus stacking**](/en/blog/focus-on-the-focus-stacking) to ensure perfect sharpness across the entire volume.

Pearlescent finishes, exfoliating grains, foams or oils… the sensory richness of cosmetic textures has to come through in the image. A **macro** lens is also recommended here, with a wide aperture (f/4 or f/5.6) to bring out the relief.

**Cross-polarization** can be very useful on glossy packaging to eliminate stray reflections.

### Clothing and textiles: a balanced focal length for natural proportions

On a mannequin or bust form, textiles require faithful rendering of proportions. A prime lens between 50 and 85mm is ideal: it avoids distorting the shoulders or chest and keeps the perspective natural.

Remember to match your focal length to the size of the product and the working distance available in your studio.

### Shiny and reflective objects: controlling reflections

Reflective objects pose real technical challenges. A smooth surface acts like a mirror and reflects its surroundings, including the lens itself. Orbitvu studios include several solutions to get around this problem:

- [**Multi-source lighting**](/en/blog/product-photo-lighting) to even out the light and avoid blown highlights
- **Polarizing filters** on the light sources and on the lens (cross-polarization)
- **Precise LED positioning** to create controlled, flattering reflections

To reduce reflections even further, some photographers also use **dulling sprays** (a temporary optical powder), which tone them down without altering the product's surface.

## Product video: which lenses should you use to film products in motion?

Video has become central to e-commerce and social media strategies. Filming products requires a lens that can follow movement without losing sharpness, while providing good depth of field.

Here are a few criteria to consider:

- **Quiet, smooth autofocus**, to avoid unwanted noise in your recordings
- **Stability**, if you shoot handheld (in the studio, use a rail and a motorized turntable instead)
- **Medium or macro focal length**, depending on the type of shot

## Are there good lenses for product photography on a limited budget?

Yes, as long as you make the right trade-offs. Third-party manufacturers, as well as the camera brands themselves, now offer high-performing lenses at reasonable prices.

A few examples:

- **Sigma 105mm f/2.8 Macro**: very good value for money, effective autofocus, compatible with Canon, Nikon and Sony

Make sure, however, that the **lens mount is compatible with your camera body**, and check that the lens is **recognized by your Orbitvu software** if you work in an automated studio.

## Going further: caring for your lenses so they last

A good lens can last ten years or more if it is properly maintained. Here are the best practices to follow:

- Clean the lens elements regularly with a **soft brush and a microfiber cloth**
- Use an **alcohol-free lens cleaning solution**, applied to the cloth, never directly to the glass
- Avoid dusty or humid environments
- Store your lenses in an **airtight box**, with a desiccant pack if possible

A clean, properly stored and well-calibrated lens delivers consistent results and extends the life of your investment.

## FAQ

### Which lens produces striking visuals… without retouching?

A 100mm macro lens, combined with an automated studio such as the Alphashot Pro G2, delivers perfect sharpness across the entire product, even on complex surfaces. The result: an image you can use immediately, without retouching, in every format.

### How can you save time without compromising quality?

With a prime lens (50 to 100mm) and an Orbitvu studio, every setting can be saved: you reproduce your visuals in series without re-entering any parameters. The right lens keeps the angle constant; the studio automates lighting and framing. Maximum productivity.

### Can you combine technical precision with creative rendering?

Yes. Modern lenses (especially macro and fast lenses) reveal material, texture and relief. With Orbitvu, you can add animations, 360° videos or focus stacking: creativity and consistency, combined in a single photo session.

### Do I need to change lenses for every product?

Not if you choose a professional zoom (such as the Canon 24-105mm f/4L) and work in a multi-product environment. For precious or technical objects, however, the macro lens remains essential to render every detail without compromise.

### Is there a way to see everything… without missing a thing?

Yes: a macro lens combined with focus stacking. The camera takes a series of images, shifting focus from one plane of the product to the next, then merges them automatically. You get an image that is sharp throughout, even on large or highly textured products.
