## 1. Choose the right lens to photograph a ring

In packshot photography, the choice of **lens** is critical to getting professional-quality shots, so it deserves careful thought. A **macro lens** is ideal for photographing a ring or gemstones: it offers a **1:1 magnification ratio** without any additional accessories, while preserving the sharpness and detail of the product. With a macro lens, you can capture the finest details and create outstanding packshots.

![Canon Macro 100mm lens next to a Canon 24-70mm zoom lens on a teal background](/images/blog/67dbae7e4d8a2c44393a7fc0.avif)

That's why we recommend the **Canon 100mm macro lens**, an ideal choice for photographing rings thanks to its sharpness and its ability to render striking detail at very close range. It brings out the finest details of your rings and showcases the quality and craftsmanship of your creations. However, the **blur** caused by a **shallow depth of field** is unavoidable with a macro lens. While photographers often seek out and appreciate this effect, it can make a macro lens unsuitable for some uses. If you want an image that is sharp from front to back, use the **Superfocus** feature (focus stacking), included in the Alphashot Micro Pro v2 software, to eliminate progressive blur.

## 3. Lay the ring flat or stand it upright with clear adhesive

Once the transparent tray is in place and your camera is ready, you can lay the ring **flat** or stand it **upright** using **clear adhesive**. Unlike other solutions on the market, the Alphashot Micro Pro v2 **does not have a built-in system for holding rings** upright, which is why the clear adhesive technique is so important. Make sure the lighting is properly adjusted to avoid **unwanted shadows**. The adhesive can then be **easily removed** with the software's retouching tools, leaving your images flawless. Remember to check **alignment** and **focus** regularly to get the best possible results.

## 5. Use silver and black reflectors to control light and shadow

Getting the **right contrast** is crucial in jewelry photography. **Silver and black reflectors** let you control light and shadow around the ring and highlight what makes it distinctive, such as **set gemstones** and the finest details. They also improve the **color rendering** and textures of your jewelry, for a refined presentation of every piece.

![Exploded view of the Alphashot Micro Pro v2 with its interchangeable silver and black reflectors](/images/blog/67d05590a1bdfbab969075d0.avif)

For example, when photographing a diamond ring, angling the **silver reflector** to create bright highlights can produce an impressive **sparkling** effect. You can also accentuate the diamond's **facets** and capture plays of light that enhance the elegance of the piece, bringing out the brilliance and purity of the stones under different lighting conditions. Conversely, when photographing a ring with darker tones or in matte metal, using a **black reflector** to deepen the shadows can add **depth** and a touch of mystery to the final image. You create **striking contrasts** that reveal the subtle details of the materials while adding a dramatic touch to the whole composition.

## 6. Adapt the lighting for special stones such as amber

For any product photographer, amateur or professional, **light** is the most important tool after the camera. The goal is to produce a series of photos that is **consistent** and **true to life**. When you photograph a ring, some stones, such as **amber**, can react poorly to intense lighting, so it is essential to adapt your approach. Avoid lamps that could mask the natural beauty of these stones, and try different **lighting settings** in the software to find the most flattering setup.
