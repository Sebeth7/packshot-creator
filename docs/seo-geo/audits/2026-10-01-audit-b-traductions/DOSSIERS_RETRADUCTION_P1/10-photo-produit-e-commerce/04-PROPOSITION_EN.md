# E-commerce product photography: 10 tips for better visuals

Looking to **increase sales** on your website? The **quality of your visuals** can play a decisive role. A **well-thought-out product photo** does more than present an item: it makes it **desirable**, **clear** and **engaging**.

Here are **10 proven tips** to improve your product photography for e-commerce, whatever your industry or your level of photography experience.

## 1. Follow the core principles of packshot photography in your e-commerce product photos

![E-commerce product photo of a yellow armchair on a white background](/images/blog/67dbae73b8c0e26556f4e206.avif)

For any e-commerce product photo, whether in the [furniture industry](/en/industrie/mobilier-decoration) or elsewhere, it is essential to follow the best practices of packshot photography. **Here are some of its core principles:**

- Show **the product accurately**, without **unnecessary embellishment**
- **Avoid misleading retouching**: a customer who is disappointed by the real thing is not a loyal customer
- Use a **neutral white background** to keep the focus on the product
- Control **the lighting** with a **neutral color temperature** to ensure accurate color rendering

If you want to learn more, read our [**complete guide to packshot photography**](/en/blog/packshot-photography-guide-why-make-product-packshots), a series of articles we wrote for beginners and more experienced photographers alike. These articles cover all the technical aspects that are essential to creating quality product photos. You will also find practical tips to improve your shots and boost your sales.

## 2. Use at least 2 or 3 images per product

Online shoppers need clear, accurate information about the products they want to buy. That is why **it is recommended to provide at least two or three images per product**. Offering several photos also shows that you have complete confidence in your product. It proves to your customers that you are transparent and have nothing to hide.

This transparency **reassures shoppers**, who feel more confident when they have access to more information. In short: the more product photos you have, the better your chances of earning your customers' trust!

## 3. Don't forget to show your product from several angles

![Yellow armchair shown from three different angles](/images/blog/67dbae73b8c0e26556f4e1fe.avif)

Since your customers cannot touch or try products online, it is essential to **show your product from different angles** so they can form an accurate picture of it. This covers the technical aspects of the product, but also its design, finishes and specific details. A [**360-degree animation**](/en/studios-photo-automatises) can also add real value to your product page!

## 4. Zoom in on the details in your e-commerce product photos

![Close-up of a yellow croc-embossed leather bag: leather grain and stitching](/images/blog/67d18f947ba281b286aa3c6e.avif)

Some products, especially those with intricate details, take more work to give shoppers a complete view. So don't hesitate to take **close-ups of certain details**, for example to show the fabric of a garment or the delicate engravings on a pendant up close. The more details you can show, the better.

Zooming in on these details also helps customers **better assess the quality of your product**. For example, by showing the stitching on a garment, you let customers see that the product is well made and durable. This can justify a higher price and convince customers that the product is worth it.

## 5. Include one or more lifestyle photos

![Furniture lifestyle photo taken in a packshot studio: a woman sitting on a sofa](/images/blog/678915240464b5b6eb2a0050.avif)

Customers often need to see the product in action and understand how it can improve their lives before they buy. That's where **lifestyle photography** comes in. This type of photo **shows the product in a specific setting** to bring it to life and present it in a situation that could be real.

With a lifestyle photo, your customers can picture what their life will look like once they have bought the product. It helps them imagine owning it and see it as already part of their everyday life. Showing your product in a real-life setting also lets you highlight its **features and benefits**. It is a chance to show the problems the product solves and convince customers to buy it.

## 6. Show how the product works or how to install it

As an extension of lifestyle photos, **another idea to build into your e-commerce product photos** is to **show how the product is installed** if this is not intuitive, or **how the product works** afterwards. It is entirely possible to create a tutorial in the form of a diagram or an image sequence and add it directly to your product page.

By showing how your product works or is installed, you give your customers an optimal shopping experience, reassure them and increase your chances of making a sale.

## 7. Make your product's dimensions clear

Online, our perception can easily be misleading: **we may, for example, imagine a product to be bigger or smaller than it really is**, without the seller ever meaning to mislead anyone. It is simply hard to picture the real size of a product you only see in pictures.

That is why it is essential to give clear information about a product's size. **You can do this in several ways:**

- in a lifestyle photo by placing it next to a model or an everyday object,
- with a diagram showing its exact dimensions,
- with a size chart if you offer several options.

Giving your customers this kind of clear information can also save you a lot of returns and complaints.

## 8. Optimize your e-commerce product photos for the web

![TinyPNG home page, an online image compression tool](/images/blog/67dbae73b8c0e26556f4e203.avif)

Have you ever visited an online store and waited forever for the product images to load? Not only is this frustrating for potential customers, it can also hurt your business.

Yet this is a problem that is easy to fix: very often, all you need to do is **optimize and compress the product photos on your online store**. By reducing the file size of these images, you not only improve your site's loading speed, but can also save storage space with your hosting provider. Do not take this **image optimization** lightly: it can significantly **improve the user experience** and, in turn, increase your conversion rate.

The **JPEG format** is one of the most widely used image formats on the web, because it offers good image quality while being easy to compress. You can optimize your product photos directly in **photo editing software** such as Photoshop, or use **free online tools** such as [**TinyPNG**](https://tinypng.com/).

Having **images with the right dimensions** also matters when optimizing your website. An image that is too large will take longer to load than one that is the right size. Check the dimensions of your images before uploading them to make sure they suit your website.

## 9. Use your photos to boost your SEO

Having product photos that sell is one thing, but having an **e-commerce site that attracts plenty of visitors** from search engines is even better!

And the e-commerce product photo you are trying to optimize can also help you bring more customers to your site through **search engine optimization (SEO)**. In fact, **your images can have a significant impact on your site's SEO**. **Here's how to use your product photos to boost your SEO:**

- Fill in the **image alt text**, describing what the image shows and including relevant keywords. Google uses these descriptions to understand the content and context of the image.
- **Rename your image files** descriptively: this also makes it easier for search engines to index them.
- Submit an **image sitemap** to search engines so they can find your images easily, which improves your chances of ranking (many WordPress SEO plugins, such as [**Yoast SEO**](https://wordpress.org/plugins/wordpress-seo/) and [**Rank Math SEO**](https://wordpress.org/plugins/seo-by-rank-math/), make this step easier).
- **Optimize the size of your images**, as explained in the previous tip (page loading speed is an important factor for Google!).
