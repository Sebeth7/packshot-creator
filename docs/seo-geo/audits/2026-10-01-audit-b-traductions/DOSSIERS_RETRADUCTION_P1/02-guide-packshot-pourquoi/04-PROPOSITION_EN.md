Welcome to our complete guide to **packshot photography**, a series of articles designed to help you take **attractive photos** of your products and **optimize your online presence**. If you sell products online, you may be surprised by how much of a difference good packshot photography can make.

When potential customers land on your website, the first thing they see is an image of your product. If that image is poor or fails to show your product in its best light, they are unlikely to buy from you. Conversely, **high-quality packshot photography** can catch shoppers' attention and encourage them to buy.

In this first article, we explain what packshot photography is and what it can do for you.

## What is packshot photography?

### Packshot definition

By definition, **packshot photography** is a **specialized photographic technique** that consists of photographing products isolated on a neutral background, particularly for e-commerce sites.

![Packshot of a gray tote bag on a white background](/images/blog/67dbae71504f03ad3bf5ac89.avif)

In practice, packshot photographs are **essential** for any business that sells its products on an e-commerce site: a good packshot can make the difference between a completed sale and a customer who shops elsewhere. The advantage of packshot photography is that it focuses on the product, and only the product, with no visual distractions. It also aims to **represent the product as accurately as possible**, so customers get no unpleasant surprises when it arrives.

![Packshot of blue lace-up boots with three close-ups of details: eyelets and laces, stitched sole, seams](/images/blog/67d14eadad46b55ee8628ca4.avif)

Packshots are usually taken in a [**professional studio**](/en/studios-photo-automatises), with lighting specially adapted to each product to bring out its specific features. Packshot photography therefore requires **in-depth knowledge** of light, cameras and composition to produce a clear, sharp image suited to the product. ### Where are packshot photographs used?

![E-commerce product page showing a packshot of a child's high chair, with thumbnails of its other views](/images/blog/67d14eadad46b55ee8628cb6.avif)

Are packshot photographs only for e-commerce sites, or can they be used elsewhere?\
You may be asking yourself that very question right now. If you invest in packshot photography, you are entitled to expect to get the most out of it.\
The primary purpose of packshot photographs is to **showcase a company's products** online, and as such they lend themselves to **many uses**:

- on your **e-commerce site**, of course, where they spark customers' interest by presenting your products attractively while showing their features, colors, textures and dimensions;
- on **social media** such as Instagram or Pinterest, to expand your audience and reach new people;
- in your company's **advertising and promotional communication** about its products: ad visuals, posters, flyers, catalogs and sales brochures;
- in **internal communication** (training employees on a product's features, for example);
- or in the **instruction leaflets** you provide to your customers.

In the furniture sector, for example, detailed images can help customers appreciate the quality and design of each piece, making their purchase decision easier. A clothing store, meanwhile, can use packshot photographs to present its new collection on its website, **increase its conversion rate** and **improve its organic search rankings**, while also promoting that collection in online ad campaigns or promotional brochures. That said, the main use of packshot photographs remains the e-commerce **product page**, so that is the use this series of articles will focus on first.

## Packshot meaning & official terminology

The term **"packshot"** comes from advertising and broadcast production, where it refers to the closing shot of a commercial featuring the product and its packaging. The variant spelling **"pack shot"** (two words) is also widely used, particularly in British English and in broadcasting; both refer to the same concept. In the UK, the IAB (Interactive Advertising Bureau) and broadcast standards bodies have codified packshot requirements for digital advertising. ## Packshot photography, lifestyle photography or still life photography? Types of product photography that serve different purposes

There are several product photography techniques, each with its own characteristics and strengths. Packshot photography is one of them, alongside lifestyle photography and still life photography. So which one should you choose for your needs?

### Packshot photography

![Multi-view packshot of a full-face motorcycle helmet shown from five angles on a white background](/images/blog/67dbae71504f03ad3bf5ac90.avif)

**Packshot photography** is a technique that consists of photographing isolated products on a **uniform background**. It focuses on **product details** by removing anything that could distract customers. The goal is to **represent the product as it really is**, with no stylistic effects and no alterations to its appearance to make it look better.

### Still life product photography

![Still life: a pair of sneakers staged in midair on a light gray background](/images/blog/67d14eadad46b55ee8628cbf.avif)

**Still life product photography**, by contrast, consists of **staging the product** creatively with various props and set elements. Unlike classic packshot photography, which focuses on a frontal presentation of the product, still life leaves more room for **creativity**. By playing with composition and color, a still life photographer can add an **artistic dimension** to a company's brand image. Since the product is not necessarily represented accurately, still life photography is not really suited to e-commerce. It is, however, ideal for an **advertising campaign**.

### Lifestyle product photography

![Lifestyle photo of a watch worn on the wrist, with a blue and green fabric strap, a striped shirt and a light-colored jacket](/images/blog/67d14eadad46b55ee8628cdd.avif)

**Lifestyle product photography**, for its part, stages products in their **context of use**. Among other things, it shows customers how to use the products and gives them a sense of their size and usefulness in everyday life. It can also help you **make the product feel more real** to customers by letting them picture themselves using it day to day. Lifestyle photography is therefore used mainly in advertising. Note: [AI product photo](/en/ia-photo-produit) tools can now generate lifestyle visuals from an existing packshot, opening the way to a hybrid workflow that combines packshots and AI for e-commerce catalogs.

As you can see, packshot photography focuses on the **product itself** rather than on the environment in which it is used. Its ultimate goal is to let potential customers see and understand the product clearly, which helps them make an **informed purchase decision**. Lifestyle photography instead aims to make an **impression** on viewers by showing how the product can be used in daily life. Still life photography, for its part, aims to highlight the product's **aesthetic qualities**, the photographer's artistic vision and the brand's message.

## Why is good product photography essential?

### The internet: a new era that changes everything for product presentation

With the internet, online shopping has become increasingly common, to the point of becoming the norm. There is just one small problem: unlike in a physical store, consumers can no longer touch, examine, try on or physically experience products before buying them!

A new challenge then arises: how do you **make the product real and tangible** for consumers? How do you faithfully convey its details, colors and textures… despite the barrier of the screen?

That is the whole purpose of packshot photography, and the common thread running through this series of articles.

### Stand out from the competition by making a good impression

**Online competition** is fierce, and **first impressions** are crucial. Professional, high-quality photographs are a way to stand out and capture potential customers' attention. The reverse is even more true: nothing drives customers away like poor-quality packshots.

Knowing this, many sellers are tempted by the easy option: simply using their supplier's photos. But is that really a good idea? It may seem practical and inexpensive, but it can backfire: those same photos are very likely to appear on your competitors' sites as well.

### A key element for your e-commerce site's SEO

Photographs are also essential for **search engine optimization**, or **SEO**. Search engines like Google rank websites by relevance and quality, and high-quality product photos can significantly influence those rankings.

![Google results for the query "montre homme" (men's watch): sponsored listings of watches shown as packshots](/images/blog/67dbae71504f03ad3bf5ac93.avif)

Search engines treat images as **content**: they can be indexed and appear in search results, just like web pages. Poor packshot photographs can therefore lead to a drop in your search rankings. And let's not forget search engines' **image search**: by pairing relevant keywords with high-quality photos, you increase your chances of appearing in the results and of attracting new potential customers to your website.

## What are the main characteristics of a good product photo?

The goal of packshot photography is to present your products in a clear, accurate and informative way. That is why a packshot must have certain characteristics to be effective.

![White-background packshots of three medium-sized products: a yellow suitcase, an electric guitar and an office chair](/images/blog/67d14eaead46b55ee8628d11.avif)

### A white background

First, the background should **preferably be white**, or at least neutral. This keeps the focus on the product and sets it apart from everything else. A uniform background reduces visual distractions and makes it easier to showcase the product.

### An accurate representation

A packshot should show the product **as it is**, without trying to embellish it or misrepresent its nature. This authentic approach helps potential customers make an informed decision when buying online. By showing the product as it really is, you reduce returns and customer disappointment.

A packshot must be as **precise and sharp** as possible. It should therefore be taken with a **professional camera**, **suitable lighting** and the right settings, ideally in a **studio environment**. Lighting is key to an accurate rendering: shadows can completely change how a product is perceived. You therefore need to strike the right balance between shadows so strong that they darken the product and a complete absence of shadow, which would rob it of all depth.

### Sharpness above all

![Row of screwdriver bits: the gold bit in the center is sharp, the others are blurred](/images/blog/67dbae71504f03ad3bf5ac9b.avif)

A blurry, poorly framed or poorly lit photo can also lower the perceived quality of the product. In other words, a bad photo can cost you a sale. So eliminate all **blur** from your photos: customers must be able to see every detail of the product. [**Focus stacking**](/en/guide/how-to-do-focus-stacking-for-bracelet-photography) can help you achieve this in packshot photography. We will come back to this technique, and many others, later in this series.

### No distractions

Finally, as mentioned earlier, a packshot must be free of any **distraction**. The product should therefore be presented in a simple setting, with no objects, colors or textures in the background that could distract potential customers. The goal is for the photograph to draw attention to the product itself, and nothing else.

![Packshot of a mountain bike's front wheel on a white background: knobby tire, spokes and disc brake](/images/blog/67dbae71504f03ad3bf5ac98.avif)

## FAQ

**Q: What exactly is a packshot photo?**

A packshot photo is an image of a product isolated on a neutral background (usually white), created with a specialized professional photographic technique to present the product clearly, accurately and informatively, particularly for e-commerce. Unlike lifestyle or artistic photography, a packshot focuses exclusively on the product itself, with no contextual or decorative elements that could distract attention.\
This technique requires a thorough command of lighting, camera settings and composition to highlight the product's specific features. The fundamental goal is to represent the product as faithfully as possible, so that consumers can appreciate its details, textures, colors and exact dimensions without any misleading visual manipulation.\
Packshots are generally taken in a controlled studio environment, with carefully calibrated lighting that brings out the distinctive features of each product while minimizing unwanted shadows. This methodical approach ensures visual consistency across the different images in a product range, which strengthens the brand's visual identity and makes it easier for users to navigate e-commerce platforms.

**Q: What are the differences between packshots, lifestyle photos and still lifes?**

Packshot photography: a minimalist approach on a neutral background (usually white) that presents the product in isolation to highlight its own features. The goal is informative and commercial: showing the product as faithfully as possible. Ideal for e-commerce product pages.\
Lifestyle photography: places the product in its context of use and shows how it fits into everyday life. It tells a story and helps customers picture themselves using the product. Instead of focusing on "what" the product is, it shows "how" and "why" to use it.\
Still life photography: stages the product in an artistic composition with various decorative elements. It favors aesthetics and emotion, creating a particular atmosphere around the product. Ideal for advertising campaigns and brand communication.

**Q: What are the technical characteristics of a professional packshot?**

A professional packshot stands out through a precise combination of technical characteristics that determine its quality and commercial effectiveness. The first is resolution. [À VALIDER : voir 06, point 11.]\
Optimal resolution and image size\
A professional packshot measures at least 2,000 pixels on its longest side (ideally 3,000 to 4,000 pixels), allowing detailed zooming without loss of quality. For print, aim for at least 300 pixels per inch (ppi) at the final print size; on screen, only the pixel dimensions determine the level of detail. This high resolution lets shoppers examine the smallest details of the product, almost as closely as they could in a store. [Correction technique DPI, voir 06, point 11.]

**Q: Where does the term "packshot" come from?**

The packshot gradually moved from advertising video to professional photography with the rise of e-commerce. This shift shows how a term can travel from one medium to another:\
1980s–1990s: "packshot" is exclusively an advertising video production term, referring to the final shot of a commercial.\
Late 1990s–early 2000s: with the emergence of e-commerce, professional photographers begin to adopt this visual technique for online sales, where clear product presentation becomes essential.\
2000s: the aesthetic principles of the video packshot (neutral background, even lighting, clean presentation) are adapted to the constraints of still photography. Photographers who specialize in product photography gradually adopt the term.\
2010s–2020s: the term becomes standard in commercial photography and e-commerce. It moves away from its video origins and now mainly refers to photographs of products isolated on a neutral background. This evolution shows how professional practices adapt from one visual medium to another as technology and commerce change.
