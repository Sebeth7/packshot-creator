Welcome to the fifth and final article in our series ***The complete guide to packshot photography***. Today we're tackling a point that matters to any business that wants to stand out from its competitors online: **the consistency and uniformity of your packshot photos**. Keeping all your product photos consistent over time gives an **impression of high quality** and a **much more professional brand image**. In this article, we'll explain what to keep an eye on during your photo shoots to maintain that consistency, then walk you through the options available to make the process easier. As the saying goes, the devil is in the details!

## Consistency in packshot photography: the secret to professional photos

### Why is consistency essential for professional photos?

When you set out to sell products online, good presentation is essential to attract customers and convince them to buy: it's a point we've made again and again throughout this series. Consistency is part of good presentation… In fact, it's one of the most important principles of packshot photography as soon as you have more than one product to present and sell. If your photos aren't consistent with one another, you can quickly lose credibility and damage your brand image. Worse still, it can seriously **hurt the shopping experience and drive visitors away**. Imagine, for example, that you're shopping for shoes online and land on a website that presents its products like this:

![Shoe catalog shot from different angles against light gray or dark gray backgrounds: an example of an inconsistent photo series](/images/blog/67dbae7389928f8e5c7974b2.avif)

If each pair of shoes is shown with **different staging, inconsistent lighting and backgrounds in various colors**, you'll soon struggle to compare the products and get a clear idea of the one you're about to buy. That could put you off and send you looking elsewhere, which is perfectly understandable. This is why visual consistency matters: it makes for a pleasant user experience and makes products easier to compare. To achieve it, it is therefore **important to make sure every detail stays the same from one photo shoot to the next**, including:

- lighting
- product position
- camera settings
- background

If any of these elements changes from one photo to the next, the result can feel confusing and inconsistent. As you can see, selling products online effectively requires a **smooth, high-quality user experience**. Consistent photos for every product are a key part of that experience: they make it easier to compare products and help build a strong, recognizable brand image.

### How can you ensure consistency across all your photos for a professional result?

First, make sure **all your photos are taken under the same conditions** of lighting, focus and background. In practice, that means using the same type of light, the same focal length, the same settings and the same background for every photo. If you work with a professional photographer, make sure they know exactly what you expect and check their results during the shoot. Next, **use photo editing software to adjust** the contrast, color and sharpness of each photo while keeping the products looking natural. This lets you **standardize all your packshot photos** for a professional finish. Be careful not to over-retouch your images, though, or you risk misleading your customers.

![Orbitvu Station software: framing grid and ghost image overlay used to place each shoe in exactly the same spot](/images/blog/67d1a9b8f6428d7e0d19c7b5.avif)

Finally, use a template or grid to **place every product in exactly the same spot in the frame**. This makes cropping easier and avoids any confusion between products. We also recommend the second article in this series, on [**photo equipment and the key concepts to know**](/en/blog/packshot-photography-guide-product-photography-equipment): there we went into more detail on why you need a precise plan of where your lights and equipment go, so that you can always reposition them at exactly the same distances. By following these few tips, you'll find it easier to keep your packshot photos consistent across your e-commerce site. You'll project a professional, coherent image to your customers and make them want to buy your products with confidence. That leaves us with just one question for you, and it's a big one: **how are you going to take your packshot photos?**

## Which packshot photography solution is right for your needs?

Will you outsource your packshot photography to a service provider, set up a traditional photo studio on your premises and bring in a freelance photographer, or invest in an automated photo studio? No one solution is necessarily better than the others. It all depends on how much time and money you want to put into it: **each option has its merits, but also its drawbacks**. Let's look at them one by one.

### Solution 1: send your products out to be photographed

The first option is to **send your products to an outside provider** specializing in product photography. This approach has some real advantages. To begin with, you don't have to worry about buying expensive camera equipment or setting aside space on your premises for the shoot. **Everything is handled off-site.** You don't need in-depth photography knowledge either. There are, however, significant drawbacks to consider. You'll have to **find a provider you can trust** and organize the **logistics** of shipping your products out and getting them back, and this **back-and-forth** can take longer. You may also have **less control** over how your final photos turn out, unless you spell out your expectations and requirements in a detailed brief for your provider. Either way, this option can be worthwhile for businesses that are new to packshot photography and need a quick, practical way to produce quality images of their products.

### Solution 2: set up a traditional photo studio and hire a freelance packshot photographer

If you're looking for a more tailored way to photograph your products, you might consider **setting up a traditional photo studio on your premises** and hiring a freelance product photographer. Although it may seem expensive at first, this option has its advantages.

![Traditional photo studio with a white backdrop, softboxes on stands and overhead lights](/images/blog/67d1a9b7f6428d7e0d19c75b.avif)

First, having a photo studio on your premises gives you **better control** over the shots. You can adjust the lights and props as you see fit and keep a close eye on the shoot as it happens. And because **your products never leave your premises**, turnaround is shorter and you can get them back as soon as the photos are taken. On the other hand, **the upfront cost can be high**, since you'll have to buy and install all the necessary equipment. **This can take a lot of time and money**, especially if you aren't familiar with photography equipment. A photo studio can also take up a lot of space, which can be a problem in smaller premises. You'll also need an **experienced photographer to get the most out of your brand-new photo studio**. Hiring a freelance photographer is one option, but you'll then have to wait until they're available, not to mention the additional costs. You'll also need to take the time to find the most qualified and experienced photographer to ensure a flawless end result. Setting up a traditional photo studio on your premises can therefore offer many advantages in terms of shooting and responsiveness, but it will require a bigger investment of time and money.

### Solution 3: do everything in-house with an automated photo studio

The last option: **using an automated photo studio in-house**. Not only can it be more convenient and faster, it can also prove **more economical in the long run**, even though it requires a significant upfront investment. When you hire an outside provider to photograph your products, or run an in-house studio with a freelancer, you have to factor in all the associated costs: travel expenses, studio rental, photographer's fees and so on. There is also often a time limit when you hire a provider or a freelancer, which means all the photos have to be taken and retouched within a set timeframe.

With an automated photo studio in-house, on the other hand, **you can photograph your products whenever you like**, with no additional service fees. Whether it's a photographer, someone from your marketing team or a stockroom employee, **anyone can use this type of automated studio without photography expertise**: all it takes is a [short training course on how to use the studio](/fr/academy). You can take professional photos of your products at your own pace and make sure they meet your expectations and standards. In short, with an automated photo studio like Orbitvu's, **you save time and money in the long run** and gain **greater flexibility and much better control** over your packshots, which also improves your productivity. These machines also take up **far less space** on your premises than a traditional photo studio. So even though this option costs more upfront, **you have everything to gain in the long run**.
